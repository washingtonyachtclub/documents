Ithaka is a Swan 441 sailboat that is used in support of the educational mission of the club. This document covers club policies related to this vessel. A key goal of these policies is to achieve a balance between what can be learned by sailing this vessel versus safe operations, maintenance requirements, asset preservation, and financial limitations of the club.

# Purpose

Ithaka primary purpose is to support classes covering material related to sailing on large keelboats. These classes are posted on the lesson signup page of the club’s website. Ithaka is also used to conduct an annual training class on crew overboard recovery using the Sailing Foundation Life sling. Ithaka may secondarily be used on the club’s Snooze and Cruise trips, scheduled club open houses, events that enhance the club’s image and reputation with the university and boating community, and in support of fundraising activities of the club. Ithaka may also be used in support of the operation of this vessel. Examples include, but are not limited to, moving the boat for football games, refueling, sanitary waste removal, relocation to marine repair facilities, and preparation/delivery of the boat prior to, or after, classes.

# Operations

Ithaka is managed by an Operations Committee. This committee is responsible for the operation and management of Ithaka. This includes managing the budget, scheduling classes (in conjunction with the Vice Commodore), record keeping, managing Ithaka ratings, and performing maintenance. This committee will help distribute the work across multiple volunteers and improve the effectiveness of this program.

Like other fleets, Ithaka will have a Fleet Captain that is responsible for coordinating her maintenance and serving as chair of the Ithaka Operations Committee. The Fleet Captain is elected to the position following the same procedures as other WYC fleet captains. The fleet captain is also responsible for appointing members to the Ithaka Operations Committee and for making a final decisions if the Operations Committee is tied. Ithaka Operations Committee members can remove themselves from the committee or can be removed from the committee by a commodore or a 2/3 majority vote at a general club meeting.

# Safety

Safe operations are key to our success and a fundamental duty of the club to our members. On Ithaka we have a safety culture modeled on best practices used in the industry. These include:

1. Starting each trip with a safety briefing and explanation of proper use handling of equipment.
2. Maintaining equipment in proper operating condition.
3. Conducting a hazard evaluation before beginning new activities.
4. Every crew member paying attention to and reporting safety and maintenance concerns to the skipper or watch captain.
5. Wearing proper personal protective equipment. (Anyone who cannot swim must wear a PFD while above deck and all crew must use PFDs and safety harnesses at the direction of the skipper or watch captain.)
6. Staying alert and attentive to the task at hand. Actively navigate the boat while underway.
7. Ensuring that sufficient trained and qualified crew is onboard for the planned activity.
8. Having ZERO tolerance for alcohol consumption or the use of drugs that may impair a crew member while underway. No federally illegal substances are allowed on board at any time.
9. As a federally documented vessel we comply with all applicable federal regulations.

# Ratings

Ratings are used by the club to provide an objective standard of skill, training, and ability, which document a member’s capability to perform a specific task. All ratings include a practical demonstration on Ithaka of the skill being evaluated as well as a subjective evaluation by the examiner that this skill was done expeditiously, met standards, and demonstrated good judgement. Any Ithaka Skipper may serve as an examiner for all ratings except the Ithaka Skipper rating and may solicit input from Watch Captains and other experienced crew. Safety of all participants is maintained by a knowledge that all rated crew members have the required minimum level of skills for the activity.  The Ithaka Skipper rating is obtained through nomination by an existing Ithaka Skipper at a general club meeting and confirmation at that meeting by majority vote.

Ithaka has the following ratings:

1. (Basic) Crew – Anyone without another Ithaka rating.
2. Novice Crew – Qualified to crew Ithaka during daylight hours.
3. Intermediate Crew – Qualified to crew Ithaka day and night in winds below 25 knots.
4. Advanced Crew – Qualified to crew Ithaka in all wind conditions, sea states, and inclement weather. Basic understanding of the mechanical and electrical systems on Ithaka.
5. Watch Captain – Responsible for the operation of the boat and to lead the on-watch crew. Extensive experience maintaining, troubleshooting, and operating the systems on Ithaka.
6. Ithaka Skipper – Very experienced sailor with deep experience sailing on Ithaka and other comparable boats. Responsible for the safe operation of the vessel.

Persons without an Ithaka rating may sail on Ithaka at the discretion of the skipper. Details on the requirements for each rating will be in a separate Ithaka Training Manual. Ithaka classes are designed to help crew gain the experience and skills needed to be able to earn ratings. For each proposed activity the skipper will determine the minimum required crew composition for that activity to take place.

Independent of the ratings system, each Ithaka trip will have one designated Skipper and one designated First Mate. The skipper must be someone with an Ithaka Skipper rating and is responsible for the safe operation of the vessel throughout the trip. The Skipper is responsible for determining the minimum required crew composition for an activity and for deciding on safety requirements. For example, Skippers may elect to have crew wear PFDs on deck after dark or when white caps are visible or to require the use of safety harnesses while sailing after dark. The First Mate is the second in command and becomes responsible for the vessel and all decision making should anything happen to the Skipper. The position of First Mate will be selected by the Skipper from the more experienced crew on board.

# Maintenance

Ithaka upkeep and running is dependent on both funding and volunteered time.

Ithaka is funded from a multitude of sources. Like many of the boats in the club, the general fund is used to fund the operation of Ithaka as needed and within an established budget. We have also received significant donations to the club related to the operation of Ithaka, including sails, professional services, equipment, and cash donations.

Ithaka is a recreational vessel that was constructed outside the US and thus cannot be used as a passenger vessel under US law. For all classes we are allowed to collect a donation from the participants to voluntarily share in the class’s expenses. In no case does the skipper, instructors or crew receive compensation, but rather they may contribute to the class’s expenses. A class expense may include moorage, fuel, food consumed, as well as a reasonable fee to cover wear and tear on Ithaka related to the class.

It is expected that club members who sail on Ithaka volunteer their time to help in boat maintenance. Maintenance of Ithaka takes place all year between and during classes.  Participation and involvement in maintenance activities can be used to determine class registration priority.

All maintenance concerns are to be reported to the Skipper or Watch Captain immediately. Watch Captains should communicate all maintenance concerns and work completed during their watches at their earliest convenience to the Skipper. Skippers are responsible for making sure that maintenance concerns are documented at the end of each trip.  The Ithaka Fleet Captain is responsible for coordinating maintenance work and repairs in conjunction with the Operations Committee.
